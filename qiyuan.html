Origin

A Thousand-Year Craft · A Legend on Paper

Paper cutting, also known as paper carving or window flowers, is one of the oldest folk arts in China. Its history reaches back to the sixth century, more than 1,500 years ago. After Cai Lun improved papermaking in the Eastern Han dynasty, paper cutting gradually took root. It was first used in sacrifices and funerals, then evolved into a decorative art and became an essential cultural symbol at festivals, weddings and birthday celebrations. One sheet of red paper and one pair of scissors are enough to cut out a whole world.

The Spirit of the Fingertips · Flowers from Blade and Scissors

Paper cutting techniques fall mainly into two families, cutting and carving, and are further divided into yang carving, yin carving and combinations of the two. Yang carving keeps the outline of the motif, with smooth and full lines; yin carving hollows out the background, giving the picture clear layers. With skills passed down from generation to generation, artists use scissors as a brush and paper as ink, turning flowers, birds, fish, myths, legends and auspicious patterns into exquisite works of art. Every cut carries the craftsman's heart and wisdom.

World Heritage · Passing the Flame On

In 2009, Chinese paper cutting was inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. From the bold freedom of the north to the delicate refinement of the south, from the window flowers of Shaanxi to the Miao paper cutting of Guizhou, styles vary richly from place to place. Today this ancient art is reaching the world in a new form, a bright calling card of Chinese culture that carries a nation's memory and blessings and never dies out.
